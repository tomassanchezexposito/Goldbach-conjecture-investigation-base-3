## Quantitative and logical obstruction

Let $A_X\ge c_AX$ be the one-sided major lower bound supplied under the Li–Liu hypotheses and let $B_X\le C_BX^{5/2}(\log X)^{100}$ be the global minor fourth-moment bound. The Stage 66 zero-exclusion criterion cannot be strict with zero increment energy unless
$$
m+1>B_X/A_X^2\asymp X^{1/2}(\log X)^{100}.
$$
At larger $m$, it requires $V/m$ at essentially the $X^2$ scale, with the exact constant threshold in `04_arc_budget_feasibility.md`. The global moment plus the complete-minor Gram supplies only $V/m\le(1+1/m)B_X$, which is larger by $X^{1/2}(\log X)^{100}$. The exact arc allocation theorem does not change this if the only available input is $\sum_rB_r\le B_X$.

The first invalid step in trying to infer pointwise positivity from these papers is replacing a global fourth moment, Parseval/Bessel average, or exceptional-set cardinality by a signed estimate at a prescribed residual target. Those results do not contain that quantifier. Modular admissibility and an upper-bound sieve also do not give a lower bound for simultaneous primality at the reflected positions.

## Classical inputs and source limits

The inspected papers cite, among other inputs, Maynard–Pandey–Radziwiłł prime exponential-sum estimates, zero-density bounds, Pintz's explicit formula, and the classical Goldbach exceptional-set literature (including Montgomery–Vaughan and Chen). Stage 68 did not independently reprove those results or exhaustively inspect every cited classical paper. The narrow conclusion is that the exact theorem statements above, as applied in those sources, supply no disjoint arcwise fourth-moment profile or pointwise signed minor coefficient meeting (F). This does not assert that no future combination of classical tools could do so.

A useful next theorem must state uniformity in $N\in H\cap[X/2,X]$, the weight, the rational-arc partition, the shift set, and every zero-free or distribution hypothesis, then meet the strict certificate threshold. A theorem about almost all targets, even with a strong exponent, is not that result.
