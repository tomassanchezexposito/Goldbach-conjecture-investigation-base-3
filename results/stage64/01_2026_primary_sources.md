## What the fourth moment actually controls

Let $r_w(n)=\int_0^1S_w(\alpha)^2e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha$ and let $m_w(n)$ be the same coefficient restricted to the minor arcs. Orthogonality and Parseval give exactly
\[
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}|m_w(n)|^2=\int_{\mathfrak m}|S_w(\alpha)|^4\,d\alpha.
\]
Thus Li–Liu's estimate gives $\sum_n|m_w(n)|^2\ll X^{5/2}(\log X)^{100}$ and, by the one-coordinate inequality, only
\[
|m_w(n)|\ll X^{5/4}(\log X)^{50}.
\]
The positive major-arc term is of order $X$. The one-coordinate bound is larger by $X^{1/4}$ up to logarithms. The squared norm is also larger than Stage 63 M5's $o(X^2)$ energy target. The actual theorem converts this same energy into a count of exceptional targets because each missing representation forces a minor-arc coefficient of size at least a constant times $X$; the resulting cardinality is $O(X^{1/2}\log^{100}X)$, not zero.

The Li–Liu sum is **prime-only and smoothed**. It is not the unsmoothed von Mangoldt coefficient $C_\Lambda(N)$. The major-arc formula has its own $O(X^{1-c})$ error, and replacing $\Lambda$ by primes introduces higher prime powers. None of these changes supplies the missing factor $X^{-1/2}$ in the global energy or a signed bound at one chosen target. The exact comparison and first failed implication are proved in `02_fourth_moment_to_pointwise_gap.md`.

## Audit of the claimed zero-free input

The current OpenAI repository snapshot inspected was commit `4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae`, dated 2026-10-29 in its Git history. The preprint's theorem, as used by Pintz–Althöfer and Li–Liu, is much stronger than a zeta-only zero-free statement: uniformity over all primitive Dirichlet characters and all heights is necessary. The Lean scope note says its main Dirichlet theorem is uniform over positive moduli and characters, excludes the principal pole, and does not formalize the paper's later applications. It also describes a uniform Siegel-zero gap $1-\beta\geq c/\log q$ for real primitive nonprincipal characters $q\geq3$, with unspecified $c$; this only excludes the interval immediately below 1.

The source tree contains explicit theorem wrappers such as `OAI.DirichletCharacter.LFunction_ne_zero_of_seven_eighths_lt_re` and a Hecke wrapper. Their proofs delegate to large imported assemblies. The comparator challenge `.lean` files contain `sorry`; the accompanying JSON maps those statements to the separate `OAI...` solution modules. Therefore the challenge stubs are not evidence that the mapped theorem is absent, but neither their existence nor a source-level wrapper substitutes for an independent Lean build. No Lean compilation was performed in this audit. A cited short hash `fd4aeeb2ee4f` could not be resolved as a fetchable Git ref; the live snapshot above is used for provenance, not represented as that historical pin.

Accordingly, the logical chain is recorded as **conditional on the OpenAI manuscript's zero-free theorem**. If that theorem is accepted, it supplies $\Theta=7/8<11/12$ for the Li–Liu and Pintz–Althöfer hypotheses. Even then, those papers yield exceptional-set estimates, not universal positivity.
