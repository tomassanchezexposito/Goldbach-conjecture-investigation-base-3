# Stage 63 M4 — Literature review and implication audit

## Scope and evidence standard

This review checks theorem statements and hypotheses in the full 2026 Bhowmik–Grimmelt paper and the full 2018 Pintz preprint, not merely abstracts. Bibliographic metadata was cross-checked against Crossref and the publisher DOI record where reachable. Chen's original 1973 paper was not available as a readable primary scan in this environment; its theorem is recorded in its standard form, and the bibliographic record was checked against Crossref's indexed 2002 reprint (DOI below). No claim of exhaustiveness over all current literature is made. The unresolved status of binary Goldbach is consistent with the reviewed sources.

Throughout, let $E(X)$ be the even integers $N\le X$ not representable as a sum of two primes, with the convention for the trivial small endpoint immaterial here. A theorem bounding $|E(X)|$ is a statement about a set, not a statement that $E(X)$ is empty.

## Exact obstruction in quantifiers

For each fixed target $N_0$, the logical model
\[
E(X)=\begin{cases}\{N_0\},&X\ge N_0,\\\varnothing,&X<N_0\end{cases}
\]
satisfies $|E(X)|=1\ll X^{1-\delta}$ for every fixed $\delta<1$, and also satisfies $|E(X)|<X^{0.72}$ for all sufficiently large $X$. This is not an arithmetic counterexample to Goldbach. It proves the implication “a power-saving exceptional-set bound rules out every exception” is invalid as a matter of quantifiers. A density-zero conclusion has the same limitation.

The reviewed minor-arc mechanism makes the issue concrete. It first proves an integral estimate and then uses a threshold argument to bound how many coefficients can be large. That procedure does not control the sign or size of the coefficient at a prescribed $N$. Bombieri–Vinogradov is likewise an average over moduli and does not by itself supply the target-anchored, signed correlation inequality in the Stage 62.5 ledger. No cited theorem reviewed here supplies that inequality for every residual $N$.

## Stage 62.4 dependency audit

Stage 62.4 needs a polynomial upper bound for the least prime in a reduced residue class. Its chosen class modulo $W_y$ is reduced because each prescribed residue is nonzero modulo its prime factor. Linnik gives $q_y\le CW_y^L$ and hence $\log q_y=O(y\log y)$, which is used to bound the extra factors from primes dividing $q_y-2$. Dirichlet's theorem ensures a prime exists but supplies no uniform polynomial bound in $W_y$; replacing Linnik by Dirichlet would leave that quantitative step unsupported. The construction concerns the conventional singular-series profile, not actual prime-pair counts.

## Source and verification notes

- Bhowmik–Grimmelt's article header states *Analysis Mathematica* 52 (3) (2026), 767–808, first online 2026-09-23, DOI `10.1007/s10476-026-00191-z`. The publisher PDF was downloaded and text inspected, including Theorems 3.1, 4.3, 5.1 and 8.2, Proposition 4.1, Lemma 4.2, and the proof of Proposition 8.1. The PDF's later running footer appears to contain a volume-number inconsistency; the article header and DOI metadata agree on volume 52.
- Pintz's full arXiv v2 source, `arXiv:1804.09084`, dated 2018-04-30 in the PDF, was inspected. Its Theorem 1 explicitly states an ineffective threshold and $E(X)<X^{0.72}$.
- Montgomery and Vaughan, “The exceptional set in Goldbach's problem,” *Acta Arithmetica* 27 (1975), 353–370, DOI `10.4064/aa-27-1-353-370`. Its power-saving statement and effective-$\delta$ description were checked in the 2026 peer-reviewed exposition; the primary paper was not independently downloaded here.
- Chen's indexed reprint metadata: J.-R. Chen, “On the representation of a larger even integer as the sum of a prime and the product of at most two primes,” in *The Goldbach Conjecture*, Series in Pure Mathematics, pp. 275–294 (World Scientific, 2002), DOI `10.1142/9789812776600_0021`; Crossref also indexes a 1984 reprint, DOI `10.1142/9789814542487_0019`. The commonly cited original is *Sci. Sinica* 16 (1973), 157–176. The exact 1973 primary text was not accessible for verification in this environment.
- Linnik, “On the least prime in an arithmetic progression I: The basic theorem,” *Mat. Sbornik (N.S.)* 15(57) (1944), 139–178. Stage 62.4 states precisely where its consequence is used.

No result reviewed is a binary Goldbach proof. The residual restrictions $N-1$ and $N/2$ composite do not alter any of the quantifier gaps above.
