# Stage 66 — Countermodels, residual arithmetic, and literature scope

## Falsified deductions and first invalid steps

1. **Global fourth moment implies no isolated bad target.** False as a deduction from the norm alone. For $A_X\asymp X$ and $B_X\asymp X^{5/2}(\log X)^{100}$, the finite-support abstract sequence $c(n)=-A_X\mathbf1_{n=N}$ obeys the energy budget for sufficiently large $X$. The first invalid step is treating an averaged $\ell^2$ budget as a pointwise $o(X)$ bound. This is an abstract sequence countermodel, not a prime counterexample.
2. **A global moment supplies a small shift modulus.** False. The exact multiplier has full-circle square norm $2$ for every nonzero shift. Cauchy–Schwarz gives only $\sqrt{2B_X}$ per increment, and the sharp vector bound gives $(m+1)B_X$ for $m$ distinct increments. The first unsupported step would be replacing the multiplier norm on the whole minor set by an $O(h/X)$ quantity without a localized weighted estimate.
3. **A finite wheel determines simultaneous primality or residual membership.** False. The factor $A_{210}$ is a count of residue pairs. The exact targets $210$ and $420$ have identical wheel count $48$ and identical odd singular-series factor $16/5$, but $210\in H$ and $420\notin H$ because $419$ is prime. Thus these factors alone do not encode $N-1$ and $N/2$, much less primality of reflected endpoints.
4. **A universal common-relative-error baseline for adjacent normalized targets follows from wheel normalization.** Refuted for the singular-series model in Stage 62: there are infinitely many $N\in H$ with $A_{210}(N)=A_{210}(N+2)=15$ and unbounded ratio of the corresponding singular-series factors. This is a theorem about the arithmetic singular-series function, not actual Goldbach counts. Stage 62's stronger Linnik construction also makes its three-point signed certificate negative along an infinite sequence for the model profile. Neither fact is a counterexample to Goldbach.
5. **The residual coordinate forces both reflected positions prime.** The positions $3+2a$ and $3+2b$, with $a+b=N/2-3$, give an exact reflection identity. The first invalid step is inferring simultaneous primality from the algebraic pairing, congruence admissibility, or compositeness of $N-1$ and $N/2$. No such implication is proved. The coordinate is not a statement about ternary digits or a progression of difference three.
6. **A representation-anchored estimate works at a zero target.** False: if it selects a pair in $\mathcal P(N)$, the anchor is empty when $R(N)=0$. The exact unanchored four-position identities remain valid but require new signed arithmetic estimates.

## Residual arithmetic and exceptional primes

For even $N>2$, failure of $N=p+1$ is exactly compositeness of $N-1$, and failure of $N=2p$ is exactly compositeness of $N/2$. For $N\in H$, an odd-prime representation has $p=3+2a$, $q=3+2b$ and $a+b=N/2-3$. Prime 3 is represented by $a=0$ and must not be deleted by an indiscriminate wheel argument. If one summand is 2 and $N>4$, the other is an even integer greater than 2, so it cannot be prime; at $N=4$ the only such pair is the excluded diagonal. The condition $N/2$ composite excludes $p=q$ on $H$.

The factorization information in $N-1$ and $N/2$ is real arithmetic data, but the repository contains no proved correlation theorem that converts those factorizations into a lower bound for simultaneous prime values of $3+2a$ and $N-(3+2a)$. The wheel modulo $210$ handles only 2, 3, 5, and 7. Higher-prime local factors vary without bound even when the wheel values are identical, as Stage 62 proves for the singular-series model.

## Dated external theorem audit

The following is the exact source audit inherited from `results/stage64/01_2026_primary_sources.md`, whose PDFs, versions, theorem numbers, hypotheses, and hashes were checked on 2026-11-10. - **Li–Liu**, arXiv:2610.26756v1, Theorems 1–2 and Proposition 4.1 (2026 preprint): for a specified smooth prime weight, the minor-arc fourth moment is $\ll X^{5/2}(\log X)^{100}$ and their exceptional-set bound is $\ll X^{1/2}(\log X)^{100}$. These statements are conditional on one fixed $1/2\le\Theta<11/12$ bounding real parts of zeros of every primitive Dirichlet $L$-function, including $\zeta$, uniformly over conductor and height, with their stated arc parameter and weight assumptions. The exceptional set can contain one target. The moment gives no signed fixed-target increment estimate. Their positive major-arc coefficient has additional stated hypotheses; it is not a universal Goldbach lower bound.
- **Pintz–Althöfer**, arXiv:2610.15559v1, Theorems 1 and 3 (2026 preprint): exceptional-set bounds under a common zero-free hypothesis for every primitive Dirichlet $L$-function, including $\zeta$. The exponent-7/12 result is $X^{7/12+\epsilon}$; the refinement assumes a stronger common zero bound at most $2/3$. Neither is pointwise at every even target.
- **Althöfer–Chen–Perelli–Pintz**, arXiv:2611.09386v1, Theorem 1 (submitted 2026-11-06): polynomial-index exceptional sets, using Li–Liu's moment and major-arc inputs and the same uniform zero-free assertion. The theorem is for fixed polynomial degree at least two; every displayed exponent leaves room for an exceptional index and does not cover all even integers.
- **Bhowmik–Grimmelt**, *Analysis Mathematica* 52 (2026), 767–808, Theorem 3.1, DOI 10.1007/s10476-026-00191-z: an exceptional-set result under a common zero-real-part bound $\Theta<3/4$. It is not a fixed-target positivity theorem.
- **Pintz**, arXiv:1804.09084v2, Theorem 1: the audited unconditional estimate $E(X)<X^{0.72}$ for sufficiently large $X$ has an ineffective threshold and does not rule out individual exceptions.
